DigiGrip: digital hand dynamometer for stroke recovery

Senior design, UC Davis Biomedical Engineering, 2022 to 2023, for client Dr. Carolynn Patten

A digital replacement for the analog JAMAR dynamometer clinicians use to track grip strength after a stroke. A load cell between machined aluminum grip bars feeds a conditioning circuit and an Arduino, which saves each patient’s readings for a Python app that tracks recovery over time.

  • Turned user-needs surveys into the design standards the device was tested against.
  • Machined 8+ aluminum components and built the test fixtures and data acquisition setup for verification.
  • Verified linear output from 0 to 200 lbf (R² > 0.9999) in a 0.9 lb hand-held unit, at a $535 prototype cost.

SolidWorks, milling, signal conditioning, Arduino, NI myDAQ and LabVIEW, Python

HexCube: veterinary mouth gag

BMES Make-a-Thon 2022, 48-hour medical device design competition

Most Creative Prototype award

The prompt was a device to hold an animal’s mouth open under general anesthesia during dental surgery. Our design sits outside the animal: a laser-cut acrylic frame full of hexagonal holes that anchor surgical tubing anywhere the clinician needs it, so one device fits jaws of any shape or size.

We designed for easy manufacturing, cutting the frame from a single acrylic sheet and 3D printing the anchor clips and pressure gauge in ABS. HexCube was one of 3 of 15 designs picked to prototype after day one.

SolidWorks, laser cutting, FDM 3D printing (ABS), DFM

Tessellate: NICU wire management

BMES Make-a-Thon 2023

Most Promising Design award

NICU babies are surrounded by monitoring and life-support lines that tangle into clumps in a small space, creating a hazard for both clinicians and infants. Tessellate is a set of modular units that clip onto the sides of the bed and connect to each other, so clinicians can route any number of wires in any direction.

SolidWorks, modular design, user-needs analysis

Custom contact lens point cloud generation

Project intern, LV Prasad Eye Institute, Dec 2020 to Mar 2021

The institute makes custom asymmetric contact lenses on a high-precision CNC machine that needs a dense 2D point cloud as input, but a clinician’s eye scan produces only a few dozen points.

I wrote a MATLAB program that interpolates those sparse scan lines, projects them into 3D, and combines them into a point cloud of 3,000+ points dense enough to cut a lens directly. I then ran the CNC to fabricate prototype lenses from the output.

MATLAB, spline interpolation, CNC machining

Bluetooth pulse oximeter

Final project, microcontroller lab course

A wireless pulse oximeter built from an Arduino Uno R3, a MAX30102 optical sensor, and an HC-05 Bluetooth module. Firmware on the Arduino processes the raw sensor signal into heart rate and SpO₂ and streams the readings to a smartphone.

Arduino (C++), MAX30102, HC-05 Bluetooth, Fritzing

Machined phone microscope

Manufacturing Techniques for Biomedical Engineering course

A simple microscope that magnifies through a phone camera, made using four machines. I milled the body from a single aluminum block, turned the working-distance adjustment screws on a lathe, drilled the multi-size aperture plate on a drill press, and laser-etched the center plate.

Mill, lathe, drill press, laser cutter
